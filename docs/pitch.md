# Dubash

### The interpreter that would rather say nothing than say the wrong dose.

**Tuesday, 10:40 am, Nalgonda.** Dr. Arjun is from Kochi. Lakshmi is 62 and speaks Telugu. Between them: forty
patients waiting, no shared language, and a ward boy who "knows a little English". Dr. Arjun says, *"Hold the metformin
if you are vomiting."* He means **stop**. Word for word, it comes out as **keep holding on to it**.

That sentence is the whole problem. In a survey of 500 Delhi outpatients, 75.2% could not read their own
prescription. A 2026 preprint put AI interpreting at about one clinical error in three, against one in twenty for
trained humans. Speed was never the hard part. Being right is.

---

**Dubash is one phone on the table.** The doctor's half faces the doctor. The patient's half is upside down, in their
script, facing them. Gnani **Prisma** hears both and tells them apart by language. Gnani **Timbre** answers the
patient in *their doctor's own voice*, cloned from ten consented seconds. Nine Indian languages, both directions.

What makes it different is everything it **refuses** to do:

| It will not… | Because… |
|---|---|
| speak a dose it cannot verify | Every number must survive translation, checked in code. One retry, then the turn is **held**. |
| trust its own ears on a prescription | Drug, strength and timing are **read back** to the doctor first. One tap. |
| guess at shorthand | *Hold, BD, SOS* become plain instructions first. If two readings are possible, it **asks**. |
| forget what she said | Her penicillin allergy stays **pinned**. Amoxicillin is stopped before she hears it. |
| assume she understood | She says the plan back. Dubash scores it and **asks again** about only what she missed. |
| stop working at the door | At home the slip **answers the family's questions** and **checks the medicine strip**. At the next visit it hands the new doctor her allergies. No database: it was all in the QR. |

Asked something the slip does not contain, it says one sentence: *"Please ask your doctor."*

---

**Why this wins.** Everyone can translate. Dubash is a safety system that happens to translate: the model proposes,
plain code disposes. It runs on Gnani's voice stack end to end, and the whole consult replays in a browser with no
keys and no microphone.

**What it is not.** Not a medical device, and not for consent, bad news or a crisis: those need a human. Synthetic data only.
